# Phase 2 — ANCHOR (Price Negotiation)

> **Goal:** Confirm details, re-summarize pain, walk through low comps to reset price expectations, trial close with a range, and set up the Deliver call. This is where you WIN the negotiation before the novation pitch ever happens.

> **Mindset:** Speak with confidence but deliver the comp news with empathy and reluctance — you're on THEIR side, the data is just the data.

---

## STEP 1 — RE-INTRODUCTION

**Say:**
> "Hi [Seller Name], this is Carlos with Equity Path Offers, how are you?"

`[COACHING: Match their energy. If they sound upbeat, be upbeat. If they sound tired or stressed, be calm and steady.]`

---

## STEP 2 — CONFIRM CONDITION & REPAIRS

**Say:**
> "I just spoke with the underwriting team and I'm still waiting on a final decision — they're a little backed up because we have quite a few properties we're considering at the moment."

> "If it's ok with you, I'd like to briefly go over the information I have just to make sure we have everything correct."

**Confirm:**
> "I have the property address as [Address]. It's a [X] bedroom, [X] bath property that is [X] square feet per the county records."

**Then summarize the good, bad, and ugly from Call 1:**
> "From what you shared with me, the property [summarize condition — roof age, HVAC, kitchen, flooring, any repairs needed]..."

`[COACHING: Check for understanding. Give them time to elaborate or correct. Any new info about repairs = more ammo for your comp discussion.]`

---

## STEP 3 — RE-SUMMARIZE SITUATION

**Say:**
> "And the reason you're selling the property, you had said [restate their situation from Call 1]."

*(Seller confirms)*

> "And you were looking to have everything taken care of [restate their timeline — ASAP, 30-45 days, before a specific date]."

`[COACHING: This may re-open the emotional conversation. If they start going deeper into pain points, LET THEM. Be empathetic, show genuine concern, ask follow-up questions. This is where trust gets built.]`
`[🔥 Any new motivation or pain = FLAG IT]`

---

## STEP 4 — SETUP FOR COMPS

**Say:**
> "Just so you know, we have an underwriting department that will ultimately make the decision on if we're able to make an offer. They have a process that's very similar to the Fannie Mae appraisal guidelines that banks and lenders use — looking at properties in the immediate area with similar square footage, bedrooms and baths, and giving weight to the most recent."

> "Of course, most investors are normally looking for properties they can buy at a discount with cash, renovate, and then sell on the market for a profit."

> "I was taking a quick look in the area and noticed a few recent sales to take note of, just to give some facts of the area."

`[COACHING: You're framing yourself as the neutral messenger — just sharing data, not attacking their price.]`

---

## STEP 5 — WALK THROUGH LOW COMPS

**For each comp, say:**
> "Are you familiar with [Street Name]?"

*(If yes)*

> "I see a property there on [Street] that recently sold. It's a [sq ft, year built, beds/baths, similar condition], and actually ended up selling for $[LOW PRICE]."

**Key tactics:**
- Emphasize similarities to their property (same size, same age, same area)
- For on-market sales, calculate what the seller ACTUALLY walked away with after closing costs (6% agent commissions + 2-3% closing costs)
- Note days on market if it exceeds their timeline
- If they bring up higher comps, walk through ALL the upgraded features that justify the higher price (remodeled kitchen, new roof, pool, etc.) and estimate the cost of those upgrades

**Example reframe for higher comps:**
> "That one on [Street] did sell for more, but if you look at it, it had a completely remodeled kitchen with quartz countertops, new cabinets, tile backsplash, stainless appliances... a remodeled bathroom with walk-in shower and new vanity... new luxury vinyl plank throughout, new roof, new A/C, and professional landscaping. That's easily $40-60K in renovations. So when you back that out, the base value of the home without those upgrades is actually pretty close to what we're seeing."

`[COACHING: This is the CRUCIAL part of the negotiation. Handle with care. Don't throw it in their face or try to "prove them wrong." Be reluctant and empathetic — "I know this isn't what you were hoping to hear, but I want to be straight with you."]`
`[💰 You're anchoring their expectations LOW before the novation pitch in Call 3, where you'll offer them MORE than the cash offer. The contrast sells the deal.]`

---

## STEP 6 — MAGIC PROBLEM (DEEPER DIVE)

**Say:**
> "One more important question — in the spirit of helping any way we can to make this process easy and pleasant for you — I wanted to ask, besides agreeing on price, what are some other important things for you in regards to selling your house?"

> "Timeline? Certainty? Anything you may need help with or are looking to avoid?"

*(Listen)*

`[🔥 This is your second pass at the Magic Problem. They often reveal MORE on Call 2 because trust has been built. Capture everything.]`

---

## STEP 7 — POSITION YOURSELF

**Say:**
> "Thank you. Just so you know, as I said before, my job is basically to gather all the information about the property and see if there's any way to make a win-win situation for everyone. I have the seller on one side pulling on one arm, and the underwriting team and the data on the other side, and I'm the one trying to hold it all together and put the deal together so it works for everyone."

`[COACHING: This "man in the middle" framing is powerful. You're their advocate fighting against the numbers — not the enemy.]`

---

## STEP 8 — TRIAL CLOSE (SOFT PASS)

**Say:**
> "I can let them know about our conversation and see if there's a way to make this work. Do you think, hypothetically, if they were somehow able to get an offer approved in the $[LOW] to $[HIGH] range — would there be any way we could put this deal together and make this work?"

`[COACHING: The range should be at least $30K spread between low and high. Low = your cash offer number. High = still profitable for novation.]`

*(Wait and listen. Let them talk. 3-second pause after they finish to make sure they've said everything.)*

`[This is a TRIAL CLOSE — it tests their temperature. Each response tells you where to go next:]`

### If they say YES or seem open:
→ Move to Step 9 (Setup for Callback)

### If they say NO but don't give a price:
**Say:**
> "Ok, no problem. And just so I know — if we were able to make an offer, and it was sort of an 'ideal situation' where you didn't have to lift a finger or spend a penny, you didn't have to fix anything or deal with any hassle, and we could close on a timeline that worked for you — what would you say would be your 'rock bottom' for an offer like that?"

### If they give a price that's still too high:
**Say:**
> "I hear you, and I appreciate you sharing that with me."

**Then try alternative angles:**
> "Have you considered renting it out if you aren't able to get the price you're looking for?"
> "Have you considered maybe doing some work on the property and listing it on the market?"

`[COACHING: These alternatives expose whether they're truly motivated or just fishing. A motivated seller will push back on these — "No, I need to sell." That's your green light.]`
`[⚠️ If they're genuinely not motivated, it may be a pass. Don't force it.]`

### If they give a number that works:
→ Move to Step 9

---

## STEP 9 — SETUP FOR CALLBACK

**Say:**
> "Ok, I appreciate you trying to work with me. As I mentioned, I'm just a glorified information gatherer — I'm not sure where exactly they're going to land. We have quite a few properties we're looking at right now, but I'm going to go to bat for you and see if there's any way to put this together and get an offer approved."

> "I do my best to help any way I can either way."

> "I want to try to get a final decision right away. I'll get in touch with them as soon as we complete this call. Are you going to be available in the next 30 minutes or so?"

*(Pause for answer)*

> "Ok, thank you. I appreciate you sharing everything with me today. I really want to try to make this work — I just need to see if maybe there's some sort of creative option I can use to put this all together."

> "If you can give me just a few minutes, I'll get back in touch with my team and try to push this file to the front of the line and see if there's any way I can get an offer approved here."

`[COACHING: "Creative option" is foreshadowing the novation pitch in Call 3. They won't know it yet, but you're planting the seed.]`
